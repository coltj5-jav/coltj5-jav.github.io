
#### Core idea
	Just like the things you see don’t hold true meaning, neither do the thoughts in your mind.

#### What Its Teaching Us
This lesson introduces the idea that your thoughts are not *capital-T* truth.

They’re just more of the same: projections, assumptions, and stories you've made up.

The Course is gently helping you detach from your thoughts. Most of us believe everything we think, but this lesson shows you that just because a thought appears in your mind doesn’t make it true or meaningful.

**You’re learning to observe thoughts without identifying with them.**

Just as the outside world is filled with things you’ve given meaning to, the inner world is also filled with noise,  and that noise isn’t you.

This opens the door to a peaceful mind: one that no longer clings to every passing thought.

It reveals that our thoughts, like physical perceptions, have no inherent meaning beyond what we give them. We begin to dis-identify from our stream of thoughts, creating distance between our true self and mental chatter.

**Recognizing the meaninglessness of thoughts is a step toward mental freedom and inner peace.**

The aim here is to train you in the first steps toward the goal of separating the meaningless from the meaningful. It is a first attempt in the long-range purpose of learning to see the meaningless as outside you, and the meaningful within.

**Observe thoughts without judgment, attachment, or resistance.**

This practice weakens the power our thoughts have over us, particularly negative or fearful ones. This lesson prepares us to later distinguish between ego-based thinking and the true thoughts that reflect reality.    

#### How to Apply It Today
Sit quietly and let your thoughts come and go. You don’t need to control them or change them.

As you notice a thought — any thought — say:

> *“This thought about [topic] does not mean anything. It is like the things I see in this room.”*

You can apply it to neutral thoughts or intense ones — the point is to treat all thoughts the same way, without judgment. 

Do this during quiet moments or whenever you're aware of mental chatter.

#### Food for Thought
- What kinds of thoughts tend to feel “real” or “true” to me?
- What happens when I stop believing every thought?
- Can I create space between the thought and my reaction?
