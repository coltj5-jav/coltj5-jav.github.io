
#### Core Idea
	You are the one assigning meaning to everything – every object, person, event, or situation. 

#### What Its Teaching Us
- ***"I have given everything I see all the meaning that it has for me."***    
	- This may immediately trigger judgment or resistance, with thoughts like "that's crazy" or "things just are what they are."
	- This is a moment of empowerment — but also humility.
- **Understanding this principle begins to weaken the grip of automatic thoughts and judgments on your mind.**
	- Meaning doesn't reside in objects themselves – but comes entirely from your own mind's projections.
	- Your thoughts give everything its meaning — not reality, not God, not Truth.
	- It’s not that things are meaningless… it’s that their meaning comes from your own mind.
	- This realization is empowering - if you gave the meaning, you can also choose to give it a different meaning. Your interpretations are not facts but mental constructs that can be changed.
- **The goal isn’t to make you feel guilty for your perceptions — it’s to help you become aware of them, so you can choose to see differently.**
	- You’re seeing the world through a lens of your past experiences, beliefs, fears, and desires. 
	- This lens is not neutral. 
	- It colors how you interpret everything. This is what the Course is gently starting to undo.
- **The "for me" part is important because each person's experienced universe is personal to them.**
	- The lesson begins with the strong declarative "I have given" to emphasize both your responsibility for what you see and your power, highlighting the "I AM presence" beyond materiality.
- **Practice involves acknowledging your authorship of meaning for various objects throughout the day.** 
	- It should be done without discriminating between objects—not focusing only on favorite things, expensive items, or things with traditional meaning.

#### How to Apply It Today
- Just like yesterday, look around slowly and apply the idea to each object:
	- *“I have given this [object] all the meaning that it has for me.”*
	- *“I have given that [thing/person] all the meaning that it has for me.”*
- Do it casually and without pressure. You're simply observing how you are the one assigning meaning — not the thing itself.    
- Try to practice it a few times throughout the day.

#### Food for Thought
- What is something I’ve given a lot of meaning to lately? 
- How might I be seeing it through my own past or filters? 
- What would it feel like to let that go, even briefly?
